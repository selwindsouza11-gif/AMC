Warm-up

03

Square Divisors

Number Theory

How many positive divisors of 28 · 35 are perfect squares?

AMC 10

Hint: In a perfect square, every prime's exponent is even.
Answer: 15. The exponent of 2 can be 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 (5 choices); the exponent of 3 can be 0, 2, or 4 (3 choices). Thus there are 5 × 3 = 15.
04

A Mixed Committee

Combinatorics

A committee of 3 is chosen from 4 adults and 5 students. How many committees include at least one adult and at least one student?

AMC 10

Hint: Count all 3-person committees, then subtract the all-adult and all-student committees.
Answer: 70. There are C(9,3) = 84 committees total. Subtract C(4,3) = 4 all-adult and C(5,3) = 10 all-student committees: 84 − 4 − 10 = 70.
05

A Reciprocal Power

Algebra

If x + 1/x = 3, what is the value of x4 + 1/x4?

AMC 10/12

Hint: Square x + 1/x to find x² + 1/x², then square that result.
Answer: 47. First x² + 1/x² = 3² − 2 = 7. Then x⁴ + 1/x⁴ = 7² − 2 = 47.
06

Inradius from Sides

Geometry

A triangle has side lengths 13, 14, and 15. What is its inradius?

AMC 10/12

Hint: Find the area with Heron's formula, then use area = inradius × semiperimeter.
Answer: 4. The semiperimeter is 21 and the area is √(21 · 8 · 7 · 6) = 84. Since area = r · 21, the inradius is 4.
07

Same-Color Draws

Probability

A bag contains 5 red, 4 blue, and 3 green marbles. Two marbles are drawn without replacement. What is the probability they have the same color?

AMC 12

Hint: Count all same-color pairs, then divide by the number of ways to choose any 2 marbles.
Answer: 19/66. Favorable pairs: C(5,2) + C(4,2) + C(3,2) = 19. There are C(12,2) = 66 pairs total.
08

Remainder Conditions

Number Theory

What is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by 7 and leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by each of 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6?

AMC 12

Hint: The first five remainder conditions mean n + 1 is a multiple of lcm(2, 3, 4, 5, 6) = 60. Then use divisibility by 7.
Answer: 119. Write n = 60k − 1. Modulo 7, this gives 4k ≡ 1, so k ≡ 2 (mod 7). The smallest positive choice is k = 2, giving n = 119.
09

No Matching Neighbors

Combinatorics

How many distinct arrangements of the letters A, A, B, B, C, C have no two identical letters adjacent?

AIME

Hint: Use inclusion-exclusion on the three events that a matching pair is adjacent.
Answer: 30. There are 6!/(2!)³ = 90 arrangements total. Each chosen pair adjacent gives 5!/(2!)² = 30; each two-pair intersection gives 4!/2! = 12; all three pairs adjacent gives 3! = 6. Inclusion-exclusion gives 90 − 3·30 + 3·12 − 6 = 30.
10

Digit Strings

Combinatorics · Number Theory

How many 10-digit strings using digits 0 through 9 (leading zeros allowed) have digit sum 10 and no two 0s adjacent?

AIME · Final challenge

Hint: Let k be the number of nonzero digits. Their values are k positive integers summing to 10. Choose the zero positions so no two are adjacent, then multiply the counts and sum over k.
Answer: 11,257. For k nonzero digits, their values can be chosen in C(9,k−1) ways. The 10−k zeros can be placed with no two adjacent in C(k+1,10−k) ways. Summing C(9,k−1)C(k+1,10−k) for k = 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 gives 756 + 4410 + 4704 + 1296 + 90 + 1 = 11,257.
